## Priority 1: Critical Additions (High Impact, Feasible)

### 1.1 Network Topology Section

The app mentions loops and trees in several places but never systematically develops the concept. This is arguably the most important conceptual gap.

```
PROPOSED SECTION: "Topology — Trees for Gravity, Loops for Pressure"

Content:
• Why gravity makes trees (physics of convergence)
• Why pressure makes loops (reliability, fire flow, water quality)
• How topology affects solver choice (sequential KW vs simultaneous GGA)
• How topology affects failure mode analysis (cascade vs redistribution)
• How topology affects calibration strategy (sequential vs simultaneous)
• Network classification algorithms (cycle detection, bridges, articulation points)
• Loop density metrics and what they mean for EPANET reliability

Interactive feature: Topology Explorer (build a network, classify it,
simulate failure, see how calibration differs by topology)

Placement: New section between "The Big Flip" and "Objects Map"
(or between "Which Tool?" and "Force Mains")

Rationale: The app's title is "The Rosetta Stone" — the topology 
insight is the GRAMMAR of the Rosetta Stone. Without it, users 
understand the vocabulary (objects, equations) but not the 
sentence structure (how the network shapes everything).
```

### 1.2 Reverse Direction Section (EPANET → SWMM5)

The app is currently a one-way Rosetta Stone (SWMM5 → EPANET). Many users arrive from the EPANET side — water utility engineers who need to model stormwater for the first time.

```
PROPOSED SECTION: "EPANET to SWMM5 — The Other Way"

Content:
• What to UNLEARN: full pipes, demand-driven, quasi-steady
• What transfers directly: head concept, mass balance, roughness physics
• What's NEW: hydrology, subcatchments, Courant condition, Froude number
• Common mistakes EPANET experts make in SWMM5
• Setting routing timestep (the #1 EPANET-expert mistake)
• Understanding depth output instead of pressure output
• Calibrating with flow hydrographs instead of pressure points
• When surcharging is NORMAL (EPANET experts panic when they see it)

Key gotchas:
• "Why does my model take 3 hours to run?" → Courant condition
• "Why is there flow in the opposite direction?" → backwater
• "Why is my pump not working?" → pump curve in depth not pressure
• "What is this 'subcatchment'?" → it's your demand, but computed from rain
• "Why do I need rainfall data?" → because the water doesn't come from a reservoir

Placement: After "First Principles Deconstruction" or as an alternative 
entry point (choose your direction: SWMM5→EPANET or EPANET→SWMM5)
```

### 1.3 Valve Types Deep Dive

The app maps SWMM5 orifices/weirs to "Valves" but doesn't explain EPANET's rich valve taxonomy. This is a major conceptual gap for SWMM5 modelers.

```
PROPOSED SUBSECTION: "Valve Types — What SWMM5 Has No Equivalent For"

Content:
• PRV (Pressure Reducing Valve): limits downstream pressure
  SWMM5 analogy: like a weir that caps HGL at a fixed elevation
  Interactive: show HGL with/without PRV, adjustable setpoint

• PSV (Pressure Sustaining Valve): maintains minimum upstream pressure
  SWMM5 analogy: no equivalent
  Interactive: show upstream pressure behavior

• FCV (Flow Control Valve): limits flow to a maximum
  SWMM5 analogy: closest to a fixed-opening orifice
  Interactive: show flow limiting behavior

• TCV (Throttle Control Valve): variable minor loss coefficient
  SWMM5 analogy: partially open orifice with adjustable K
  Interactive: show headloss vs. K value

• PBV (Pressure Breaker): forces fixed pressure drop
  SWMM5 analogy: none

• GPV (General Purpose Valve): user-defined headloss curve
  SWMM5 analogy: closest to a custom rating curve (OUTLET)
  Interactive: draw headloss curve, see behavior

• Check Valve (CV pipe status): prevents reverse flow
  SWMM5 analogy: flap gate

Interactive feature: For each valve type, show a simple 3-node 
network with the valve, and let users adjust the setpoint/parameter 
to see how it modifies the HGL.

Placement: Expand the "Objects Map" section, or create a new 
subsection under "Objects Map"
```

```
PROPOSED SUBSECTION: "Fire Flow — EPANET's Critical Design Case"

Interactive: Fire flow calculator

  - Set target residual pressure (20 psi default)
  - Tool iteratively increases demand until pressure drops to target
  - Reports: available fire flow = X GPM
  - Shows system head curve on pump curve plot

Placement: New subsection under "Which Tool?" or under "Demands"
```

## Priority 2: Important Enhancements (Moderate Impact)

### 2.1 Pressure-Driven Analysis (PDA) Expanded

The app mentions PDA in one paragraph. This deserves deeper treatment because it's the EPANET 2.2+ feature that most directly parallels SWMM5's capacity-based analysis.

```
PROPOSED EXPANSION OF "Demand-Driven vs Pressure-Driven Analysis"

Current content: One paragraph explaining DDA vs PDA

Proposed additions:
• How to enable PDA in EPANET 2.2+
  [OPTIONS]
  DEMAND MODEL PDA
  
  [DEMANDS]
  ;;Node   BaseDemand  Pattern  MinP  ReqP  PressExp
  J1      100         Daily    10    25    0.5

• The demand function:
  D_actual = D_requested × ((P - P_min) / (P_req - P_min))^α
  where α = pressure exponent (typically 0.5-0.6)
  
  P ≤ P_min: demand = 0 (no water available)
  P_min < P < P_req: demand reduced proportionally
  P ≥ P_req: demand fully satisfied

• Interactive: show demand curve as function of pressure
  User adjusts P_min, P_req, α and sees the demand curve shape
  
• When to use PDA:
  - Pipe break scenarios
  - Fire flow under low pressure
  - Pump failure analysis
  - Pressure management optimization
  
• When DDA is fine:
  - Normal operations (all pressures adequate)
  - Design verification (checking minimum pressure)
  - Calibration (matching measured pressures)

• SWMM5 analogy: PDA is like SWMM5's surcharge behavior —
  when a junction can't accept more water, the system pushes back.
  PDA does the same for pressure: when pressure is too low,
  demand decreases. The concept is identical; the implementation differs.
```

### 2.2 Demand Allocation Methods

The app describes demands but never explains how practitioners actually assign them. This is one of the most time-consuming parts of EPANET modeling.

```
PROPOSED SUBSECTION: "How Demands Get Into EPANET — Allocation Methods"

Content:
• Unit demand methods (demand per capita, per connection, per acre)
• GIS-based allocation (assign meter data to nearest junction)
• Demand categories (residential + commercial + industrial per node)
• Demand zones and DMA (District Metered Area) concepts
• Unaccounted-for water / non-revenue water (typically 15-25%)
• How to estimate leakage (emitter coefficient: Q = C×P^γ)
• Demand diurnal patterns (residential, commercial, industrial, fire)
• Peaking factors (max day / avg day, max hour / avg hour)
• Using AMI (Advanced Metering Infrastructure) data

Interactive: Demand allocation worksheet
  - Enter total system demand (MGD)
  - Enter number of connections
  - Enter % non-revenue water
  - Tool distributes demand to nodes using selected method
  - Show resulting demand map

Placement: Expand "Demands" section
```

## The Single Most Important Improvement

If only one improvement could be made, it should be **1.1 — Network Topology Section** with its associated **3.1 — Topology Explorer**.

The app already covers WHAT differs between SWMM5 and EPANET (objects, equations, solver). It does not yet systematically cover WHY the differences exist — which traces back to the topology of the physical network. The topology section would provide the conceptual framework that connects every existing section into a unified theory:

```
TOPOLOGY (NEW)
    │
    ├──→ Solver choice (existing section: "The Solver")
    ├──→ Failure modes (existing section: "Gotchas")
    ├──→ Calibration strategy (expand existing: "Which Tool?")
    ├──→ Force main modeling (existing section: "Force Mains")
    ├──→ Design philosophy (existing section: "First Principles")
    └──→ ML architecture choice (new section: "ML Surrogates")

Topology is the META-SECTION that explains WHY everything else differs.
Without it, users understand the differences intellectually but not conceptually.
With it, they have a unified mental model that predicts any future difference 
they encounter.
```

The Topology Explorer would make this abstract concept tangible through interactivity — building networks, breaking pipes, comparing failure propagation, struggling with coupled calibration parameters. Experience teaches faster than explanation.

This is the one improvement that would elevate the app from "excellent reference" to "definitive educational platform."
