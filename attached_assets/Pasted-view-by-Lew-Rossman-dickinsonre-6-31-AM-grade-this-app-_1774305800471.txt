Grading "EPANET for SWMM5 Experts" as Lew Rossman Would
Putting on the hat of a guy who spent 30+ years at EPA Cincinnati writing EPANET and SWMM5, who named his first version "QHCSIM," who values correctness over flash, and who would read every equation before looking at a single animation.

Overall Grade: B+/A-
This is serious work. Somebody actually read the source code. That alone puts it ahead of 95% of what crosses my desk. But "ahead of most" isn't the same as "correct in every detail," and I've got notes.

What's Done Well (and I Don't Say This Lightly)
The conceptual framing is exactly right. The "fundamental flip" — gravity-driven partial flow vs. pressure-driven full flow — is the single most important thing a SWMM modeler needs to internalize before touching EPANET. Leading with this was the correct editorial decision.

The object translation table is genuinely useful. Junction↔Junction, Outfall↔Reservoir, Storage↔Tank, Conduit↔Pipe — these mappings are accurate and the "Key Differences" column captures the real gotchas. The note that EPANET junctions have zero storage volume while SWMM junctions store water is critical and often missed.

Theforcemain.c code walkthrough is outstanding. Whoever wrote this actually pulled up the source, traced throughforcemain_getEquivN(), identified the droppedSf^(-0.04) term, and quantified the resulting error at different slopes. That's the kind of work I respect — going to the code, not just the manual. The explanation that the equivalent n is "only for display and the transition zone" while SWMM uses H-W directly during full-flow routing is exactly correct and widely misunderstood.

The Preissmann slot explanation is clean. Calling EPANET pipes "all slot, no pipe" is a memorable and accurate way to describe the relationship.

The demands section nails the conceptual inversion. Water enters in SWMM, water leaves in EPANET. Same conservation of mass, opposite mental model. The mapping of DWF patterns to demand patterns with the observation that "the patterns are literally the same format" is correct and practically useful.

The CFL/Courant discussion is honest. Explaining why SWMM needs tiny timesteps (explicit scheme + wave propagation) while EPANET doesn't (implicit equilibrium solver) gets at the actual numerical analysis, not just hand-waving.

The roughness flip warning — "n goes UP for rough, C goes DOWN." I've watched practicing engineers get this wrong for decades. Beating this drum is justified.

What Needs Correction or Tightening
1. The Hazen-Williams Equation Has a Unit Error in the Interactive Calculator Context
The equation shown:

hf = 4.727 · L · Q^1.852 / (C^1.852 · d^4.87)

This is the form with Q in GPM, d in inches, L in feet, hf in feet. That's fine — but the interactive calculator also shows flow in GPM and diameter in inches, yet the velocity is computed and shown in ft/s, and the "ft/1000ft" headloss unit is displayed. The dimensional consistency needs to be verified for every calculator path. I've seen too many online tools get the unit conversion between the H-W forms wrong. If I can't verify your calculator against a hand calculation in 30 seconds, I don't trust it. Show the intermediate steps or at least display which form of H-W you're using (there are at least four common forms with different constants depending on unit systems).

2. The Gradient Algorithm Attribution Is Incomplete
You say "Todini & Pilati, 1988" — which is the seminal paper. But EPANET's implementation is based on my 2000 paper where I reformulated it for computational efficiency with the sparse matrix structure. The algorithm in EPANET is not exactly Todini-Pilati 1988; it's a specific implementation that exploits the network structure. This matters if someone tries to implement it from your description.

3. The "No Dynamic Routing — Water Travels Instantly" Gotcha Is Slightly Misleading
You state that "changing a pump status or valve setting affects the entire network simultaneously at that timestep." This is true for the hydraulic solver. But the water quality solver in EPANET does track advective transport through pipes with finite travel time. The quality timestep (typically 5 minutes) specifically exists to capture this. A reader could walk away thinking EPANET has no concept of transport time at all, which is wrong — it just separates hydraulic equilibrium (instantaneous) from quality transport (time-dependent). You do mention this in passing ("Only the water quality solver tracks travel time") but it's buried in a parenthetical that contradicts the bold claim above it.

4. "Tanks Are Cylindrical (or You Use Volume Curves)" — Incomplete
EPANET 2.2 supports volume curves for non-cylindrical tanks. But you also omit that EPANET tanks have no horizontal inflow/outflow momentum effects — the tank is a lumped volume with perfect vertical stratification (depending on mixing model). This is a meaningful limitation compared to SWMM storage units, where the inflow momentum and geometry can affect routing. The mixing model discussion is good but should note that all four models are zero-dimensional — no spatial variation within the tank.

5. The Saint-Venant Equations as Written Have a Sign Convention Issue
In your momentum equation:

∂Q/∂t + ∂(Q²/A)/∂x + gA·∂H/∂x + gA·Sf + gA·hL = 0

The friction termgA·Sf should oppose flow direction. The way it's written, with all positive signs and the equation set to zero, is one valid convention — but it's not the convention SWMM5's reference manual uses, and mixing conventions in a document aimed at practitioners is asking for confusion. The SWMM5 Reference Manual (Vol. II) writes it with the bed slopeS₀ separated from the pressure gradient∂y/∂x, not lumped into∂H/∂x. Later in Part 5 you switch to:

∂Q/∂t + ∂(Q²/A)/∂x + gA·∂y/∂x = gA·(S₀ − Sf)

which is different (and arguably clearer). Pick one form and stick with it throughout the document. Having two different forms of the same equation without explicitly reconciling them is the kind of thing that makes a reviewer twitch.

6. The Darcy-Weisbach Section Understates Its Superiority
You describe D-W as "most physically correct" and "more universal." This is true but you should be stronger: D-W is the only headloss formula with a theoretical basis (derived from dimensional analysis and the Navier-Stokes equations). H-W and Manning are purely empirical curve fits valid over limited ranges of Reynolds number, pipe diameter, and (for H-W) temperature. For any fluid other than water at ~60°F, or any pipe smaller than about 3 inches or larger than about 72 inches, H-W can give meaningfully wrong answers. I added D-W support to EPANET specifically because H-W's limitations matter in practice. The converter tool's temperature warning at 60°C is good but should note that H-W errors begin well below that threshold.

7. The Treatment Expression Editor Is SWMM-Only — Why Is It Here?
This is a SWMM5 treatment expression builder on a page titled "EPANET for SWMM5 Experts." The EPANET→SWMM direction is the stated purpose of the lower sections, but the treatment editor has no EPANET connection at all. EPANET's water quality reactions (bulk/wall decay coefficients) are fundamentally different from SWMM's node-based treatment expressions. Either connect it to EPANET's reaction model explicitly or acknowledge it as a standalone SWMM tool that's included for convenience.

8. PDA Description Could Be More Precise
You describe Pressure-Driven Analysis (PDA) correctly in concept but don't give the actual equation EPANET 2.2 uses:

D = D_f × [(P - P_min)/(P_req - P_min)]^P_exp forP_min < P < P_req

WhereD_f is the full demand,P is available pressure,P_min is minimum pressure (zero demand below this),P_req is required pressure (full demand above this), andP_exp is the pressure exponent. This is the Wagner equation form. If you're going to recommend PDA for emergency scenarios, give practitioners the actual parameters they need to set.

9. The "First Principles Deconstruction" Section Is Excellent but Has a Tone Problem
Part 6's line — "They share the word 'pipe' and approximately nothing else at the physics level" — is good rhetoric but technically wrong. They share conservation of mass, conservation of momentum (EPANET's is a degenerate form of SWMM's), Manning's equation (EPANET offers it), the concept of head as energy per unit weight, minor loss formulationK·V²/2g, pump curves, rule-based controls, and the .INP file philosophy. That's not "approximately nothing." The document's own content proves this. Don't undercut your careful translation work with hyperbole in the conclusion.

10. Missing: Accuracy Validation
There's no verification problem — no "here's a 3-pipe network, here's the hand calculation, here's what EPANET gives, here's what SWMM gives." Lew Rossman's EPANET manual includes worked examples for exactly this reason. A practitioner needs to see numbers match before trusting a conceptual framework. The "Working Example" section shows side-by-side .INP files but never shows the results and whether they agree where they should (same headloss for the same pipe at the same flow using equivalent roughness parameters). This is the most important missing piece.

Minor Issues (The Red Pen)
The pipe roughness table lists "Brick sewer" with H-W C=100 and Manning's n=0.015. A brick sewer in an EPANET pressurized context is unusual. This is a SWMM-centric entry leaking into the EPANET table.

The "Negative Demand" trick for injection wells is mentioned but you should warn that large negative demands can cause convergence issues, especially near tanks.

The decision flowchart sends "Force mains in collection?" to "SWMM5 + Force Main" — but doesn't mention that EPANET can also model force mains perfectly well (they're just pressurized pipes, which is EPANET's native domain). The force main is a pressurized pipe between a pump station and a gravity system. EPANET handles the pressurized part; SWMM handles the gravity part. Many practitioners model force mains in EPANET, not SWMM.

Typo/formatting: In the object translation table, "6.8 miles of pipe" appears in the Net 3 description context but isn't relevant here.
